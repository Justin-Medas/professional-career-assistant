# Question Bank

Pick from here when running a gap interview (onboarding Step 9) or an interview round. Never ask them all. Choose the few that fit the gap, then adapt wording to the user's actual role and phrasing. One question at a time unless batching confirm/correct items.

## Universal probes (any role)

- What were you hired to do, and what did the job turn into?
- What changed because you were there? What would have been different if you were not?
- What is the number most closely tied to your success in this role (revenue, users, time saved, defects, tickets, cost, throughput)? Roughly, with a time frame?
- What did you start, fix, or rebuild that nobody asked you to?
- Who depended on your work, and how many of them?
- What was the hardest problem and how did you approach it?
- What did you learn or change your mind about?
- Did you get promoted, expand scope, or receive an award or recognition? When and why?
- What tools, systems, or methods did you use daily?
- What work are you proud of that never made it onto a resume?

## Scope sizing

- Team: how many people, direct vs. indirect, what levels, any contractors?
- Budget or revenue: owned, influenced, or just supported? Order of magnitude is fine.
- Users or customers: how many, what type?
- Geography or org breadth: regions, business units, number of teams you coordinated with.
- Decision rights: what could you decide alone, and what needed approval?

## Leadership and management

- How many people have you hired, promoted, or managed out? Levels?
- What did you do to improve retention, engagement, or performance on your team?
- Describe a hard feedback conversation and what happened after.
- How do you set priorities and communicate them?
- What process or ritual did you introduce that stuck?

## Individual contributors (engineering, design, analytics, research, etc.)

- What did you personally build or design end to end?
- Which systems, features, or studies had the biggest user or business impact?
- What technical or design decision are you most able to defend, and what were the alternatives?
- Performance, scale, reliability, or quality numbers before and after your work?
- Mentoring, code or design review, standards, or documentation you owned?

## Product and program roles

- Which products or programs did you own, and for how long?
- How did you decide what not to build or do?
- How did you discover customer needs? Interviews, data, support, sales feedback?
- Launch results: adoption, retention, revenue, NPS, or cost impact?
- Which stakeholders did you have to align, and how?

## Sales, customer-facing, and solutions roles

- Quota or target, attainment, and time frame?
- Deal sizes, cycle lengths, segments, and notable wins?
- How did you run discovery and demos?
- Retention, expansion, or renewal results?
- Which technical or domain knowledge made you credible with buyers?

## Operations, finance, legal, HR, and other functions

- What processes did you own, and what were their volumes?
- What did you improve, automate, or standardize? Time or cost saved?
- Compliance, audit, or regulatory outcomes?
- Systems you administered or implemented?
- Vendors, budgets, or headcount you managed?

## Early career, students, career changers

- Projects, coursework, internships, volunteer work, or side projects with real outputs?
- Leadership of clubs, teams, or events: size and results?
- Skills used in past jobs that transfer to the target field?
- Anything you taught yourself recently, and what did you produce with it?

## Gaps, transitions, and special situations

- What happened between roles X and Y? How do you want it framed, if at all?
- Why did you leave each role (for your own context; you choose what appears)?
- Freelance, consulting, or side work: clients, years, outcomes, and any business name?
- Layoffs, reorganizations, mergers: what was your role in them?
- Career change: what is the narrative link between old and new?

## Freshness checks (batch these)

- Is your current title, employer, and start date still accurate?
- Any promotions, scope changes, or new projects since <date of last update>?
- Are the tools and technologies listed still ones you would claim today?
- Is your contact info still current?
- Are the target roles in `positioning.md` still the right ones?

## Story prompts (for `reference/interview-answers.md`)

- A time you made a decision with incomplete information.
- A time you disagreed with a leader or peer, and the outcome.
- A failure and what changed afterward.
- A time you influenced without authority.
- A project you would do differently now.
- A time you taught or developed someone.
- The accomplishment you most want an interviewer to ask about.
