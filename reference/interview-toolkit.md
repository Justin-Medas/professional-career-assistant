# Interview Toolkit

Reusable material for `playbooks/interview-prep.md`: question bank, follow-up templates, and checklists. Adapt wording to the user's voice (`profile/voice-and-style.md`). Templates use placeholders in angle brackets; never send without the user's explicit approval.

## Questions to ask

### Recruiter
- What does the interview process look like from here, and what is the timeline?
- Who will I meet, and what does each conversation focus on?
- Why is this role open, and how long has it been open?
- What are the hiring manager's top priorities for this hire?
- What has distinguished candidates who advanced so far?
- Is the compensation range posted accurate for this level, and how are bonus and equity structured?
- Is there anything in my background you would like me to address before the next round?

### Hiring manager
- What would success look like in the first 90 days, and at the end of year one?
- What is the hardest problem the team is working on that I would touch in my first six months?
- What was the last big decision the team made, and how did it get made?
- How would you describe your management style and what do you expect from people on your team?
- Where does this team need to improve most?
- What happened with the previous person in this role, or what is the history of the position?
- <Specific question tied to a recent company launch, post, or posting line.>

### Peers and team members
- What does a typical week look like?
- What is the best part of working here, and what do you wish were different?
- How do you handle disagreements about priorities or technical direction?
- How are new people onboarded, and what trips them up?
- What was the last thing you shipped or finished that you are proud of?

### Cross-functional partners
- How do your team and this one work together today, and where is there friction?
- What would you want from someone in this role?
- What does a great collaboration with this team look like?

### Skip-level and executives
- What are the top company priorities for the next 12 months, and how does this role connect to them?
- What does the company need to get right to win in its market?
- How do you think about <relevant trend in the sector>?
- What do you want to be true about this function a year from now?
- What makes someone successful at the senior levels here?

### For leadership candidates
- How are decisions made across functions, and where do they stall?
- What authority and budget would this role own on day one?
- How is the organization structured today, and is a change planned?
- How do you measure the health of this team?

### Closers (use at the end of any interview)
- Based on our conversation, what questions or concerns do you have about my fit?
- What are the next steps and when should I expect to hear back?
- Is there anything I should read or look at before we talk again?

### Avoid
- Anything answered on the company's website or the posting.
- Compensation, vacation, or remote-policy details with the hiring manager before an offer, unless they bring it up. Ask the recruiter.
- Questions that signal doubt about the company in a hostile way.
- "What does the company do?"

## Follow-up email templates

Keep to under 150 words. Reference one specific moment. No flattery, no repetition of the resume. Subject lines are plain.

### After a recruiter screen
Subject: Thank you, <Role> conversation

Hi <Name>,

Thank you for the time today. I appreciate the overview of the <team/role> and the process. I am keen on <specific aspect> because <one-line reason tied to the user's background>.

To confirm next steps: <summary of what they said>. Please let me know if there is anything else you need from me, including <availability / documents / references>.

Best,
<Name>

### After a hiring manager interview
Subject: Thank you, <Role> interview

Hi <Name>,

Thank you for walking me through <specific topic discussed>. Your point about <specific problem> stood out because <how the user's experience relates, one sentence with a concrete example>.

<Optional: One thing I did not cover well: ... Here is a short answer / a link.>

I am excited about the opportunity to work on <priority they named>. I look forward to the next steps.

Best,
<Name>

### After a panel or onsite (one per person)
Subject: Thank you, <Role> conversation

Hi <Name>,

Thank you for the conversation on <topic>. I enjoyed hearing how <specific detail they shared>. <One sentence connecting that to the user's work.>

I would welcome the chance to continue the conversation.

Best,
<Name>

### Following up when the timeline has passed
Subject: Following up, <Role>

Hi <Name>,

I wanted to follow up on the <Role> process. You mentioned a decision by <date>. I remain very interested and happy to provide anything else that would help.

Best,
<Name>

### Closing the loop after extended silence
Subject: <Role> at <Company>

Hi <Name>,

I understand priorities shift. I would still welcome the role if it is open, and I would appreciate an update either way so I can plan. Thank you for your time during the process.

Best,
<Name>

### Replying to a scheduling request
Subject: Re: <Interview scheduling>

Hi <Name>,

Thank you. I am available <two or three windows with time zone>. Please send the invite and let me know who I will be speaking with and if there is anything to prepare.

Best,
<Name>

### Acknowledging an offer and requesting time
Subject: Re: Offer, <Role>

Hi <Name>,

Thank you for the offer. I am excited about the opportunity. Could you share the full details in writing, and may I have until <date> to review and respond?

Best,
<Name>

### Responding to a rejection
Subject: Re: <Role> update

Hi <Name>,

Thank you for letting me know and for the time you invested. I enjoyed learning about <team/product>. If you have any feedback on how I could strengthen my candidacy, I would value it. I would welcome staying in touch for future roles.

Best,
<Name>

### Withdrawing from a process
Subject: Withdrawing my candidacy, <Role>

Hi <Name>,

Thank you for the time and consideration. After careful thought, I am withdrawing from the process for <Role>. I appreciate the conversations and wish the team well.

Best,
<Name>

## Pre-interview checklist (copy into the job's prep file)

- [ ] Posting re-read; top 5 requirements mapped to stories
- [ ] Pitch rehearsed out loud (60 to 90 seconds)
- [ ] Why this company / role / now written down
- [ ] 8 questions prepared, 3 to 5 chosen
- [ ] Interviewers researched (professional background only)
- [ ] Compensation position set (floor, target, planned answer)
- [ ] Logistics tested (tech, route, time zone)
- [ ] Cheat sheet ready (pitch, stories, numbers, questions)
- [ ] Debrief time blocked
- [ ] Thank-you drafts started

## Common behavioral questions (map each to a story)

- Tell me about yourself.
- Why this role? Why this company? Why now?
- Why are you leaving (or why did you leave)?
- Walk me through your resume.
- Biggest accomplishment. Biggest failure.
- A time you disagreed with a boss or peer.
- A time you led without authority.
- A time you handled ambiguity or changing priorities.
- A time you missed a deadline or made a mistake.
- How do you prioritize? How do you handle pressure?
- What are your strengths? What is a weakness you are actively working on?
- Where do you want to be in three to five years?
- What are your compensation expectations?
- Do you have questions for us?
