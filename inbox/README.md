# Inbox: Drop Zone

Put anything that describes your career here before or during setup. Any format is fine. The assistant reads it, extracts facts, then moves the file to `processed/`. Nothing is deleted.

Do not worry about duplicates or messy files. Conflicts between versions are expected and get resolved by asking you.

## Checklist (most valuable first)

### 1. Every resume you have, old and new
Word, PDF, Google Doc exports, plain text. Older versions often hold roles, metrics, and projects you have since cut. Tailored versions show what you emphasize.

### 2. Your LinkedIn data
Pick whichever is easiest. Doing more than one is better.

- **Full data export (best).** On LinkedIn: your profile photo menu > Settings & Privacy > Data privacy > Get a copy of your data. Choose the "Want something in particular?" option and tick Profile (positions, education, skills), Recommendations, and Connections if you want network context. LinkedIn emails a zip; unzip it and drop the CSV files here (`Positions.csv`, `Education.csv`, `Skills.csv`, `Profile.csv`, `Recommendations_Received.csv`, and similar). Menu names change over time; if one is missing, search the Settings page for "copy of your data".
- **Profile PDF.** Open your own profile > More (or Resources) > Save to PDF. Drop the PDF here.
- **Copy and paste.** Paste your About, each Experience entry, Skills, and Recommendations into a file named `linkedin-paste.md`.
- **Live read.** If you use Claude in Chrome and are signed in to LinkedIn, the assistant can offer to read your own profile page. It will ask first.

Also useful: your public profile URL, and any recommendations people have written for you.

### 3. Performance reviews, self-assessments, brag docs
Reviews hold numbers and outcomes your resume likely lacks. Strip anything confidential that you cannot share with a tool; summaries are fine.

### 4. Past cover letters, outreach messages, and anything you wrote at length
Used to learn your voice. Emails to colleagues, LinkedIn posts, and blog posts work too.

### 5. Project material
Design docs, launch announcements, slide decks, case studies, portfolio PDFs, press mentions, conference talks.

### 6. Education and credentials
Degrees, certifications, licenses, transcripts (only what you want on a resume).

### 7. Job targets
Postings you are interested in, a list of target companies, or notes on what you want next. Put them in `targets.md` or save postings as files.

### 8. Links
A `links.md` file with your personal site, GitHub, portfolio, publications, talks, or anything public. The assistant can fetch public pages.

## What not to drop here

Passwords, government ID numbers, bank details, health records. The assistant is instructed not to store these and you do not need to share them.

## Naming

Names do not matter, but if you know the date or target job of a resume, include it (`resume-2024-pm-fintech.docx`). It helps the assistant judge which version is newest.
